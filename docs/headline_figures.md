# Tshwane capital budget: headline figures

Infrastructure is counted where its users live, not where it is built; money listed only by region is spread across the region's wards by population. Figures from `data/v2_index.csv` and `data/v2_bands.csv` (service-area basis).

- **Tshwane's residents average 82.7% Black African** (Stats SA 2022 ward estimates). A budget whose money averages higher leans toward Blacker wards; lower, it leans away.
- **2022/23 – Williams (DA): 87.3% (+4.5 points); 2023/24 – Brink (DA): 87.0% (+4.2).** Both budgets leaned toward Blacker wards by a similar amount.
- **2024/25 – Brink (DA): 84.1% (+1.3).** Lower than the two years before, still slightly above where people live.
- **2024/25 adjusted – Moya (ActionSA): 81.5% (-1.2).** The mid-year adjustment to the 2024/25 budget, made under Moya; the only budget in the series below where people live. Actual spending that year (July 2024–June 2025, spanning both mayors): 83.4% (+0.7).
- **2025/26 – Moya (ActionSA): 85.7% (+2.9); 2026/27 – Moya (ActionSA): 87.8% (+5.0).** Above where people live again; 2026/27 is the budget as tabled in May 2026.
- **Mid-year adjustments moved the number by +1.3 (2022/23), +1.5 (2023/24), -2.6 (2024/25) and -1.9 points (2025/26; excludes a R13m Refilwe roads item missing from the City's project list).** Wards that are 80–100% Black African are home to 75% of residents; only 38–53% of each budget could be linked to specific areas.

_Capital budgets only (not day-to-day services). About 38–53% of each budget could be linked to specific areas. Source: City of Tshwane budget documents and annual reports 2022/23–2026/27; Stats SA 2022 ward estimates._
